\begin{abstract}
We present a comprehensive, multi-dimensional mathematical landscape of the Riemann Hypothesis (RH), establishing the strict mathematical equivalence among five major modern frameworks: (1) self-adjointness and real spectrum of the continuous screw-operator limit $D_\infty = \lim_{a\to\infty} A_a$ (Operator Algebra); (2) strict positivity of the logarithmic derivative real part $\operatorname{Re}(\xi'/\xi(s)) > 0$ across the open half-plane $\mathbb{H}_{1/2} = \{\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1/2\}$ (Complex Analysis); (3) positive semi-definiteness of the Weil explicit quadratic functional $Q_W(g) \ge 0$ on the complete admissible space $\mathcal{T}_{\text{Weil}}$ (Harmonic Analysis); (4) the uniform Archimedean barrier $-\operatorname{Re}(\zeta'/\zeta(\sigma+it)) \le \frac{1}{2}\log(|t|/2\pi) + \mathcal{O}(1)$ for all $\sigma > 1/2$ (Analytic Number Theory); and (5) the global Schwarz--Pick hyperbolic contraction metric $\mathcal{S}(\sigma, t) = \frac{(\sigma-1/2)|F'|}{\operatorname{Re}(F)} \le 1$ with saturation $\mathcal{S} \to 1.000000$ at the critical boundary (Geometric Function Theory). We prove the Hyperbolic Non-Continuation Theorem, demonstrating why local contraction in $\operatorname{Re}(s) > 1$ cannot be softly propagated across the critical strip without full arithmetic Euler-product rigidity. Furthermore, we dissect the exact mechanism of the off-axis cross-pairing term $-\sum \widehat{v}(\rho)\overline{\widehat{v}(1-\overline{\rho})}$ and provide high-precision quantitative contrast with the metric tearing and Krein-space degeneration observed in Epstein zeta functions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Riemann Hypothesis, formulated in 1859, asserts that all non-trivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function $\zeta(s)$ satisfy $\operatorname{Re}(s) = 1/2$. Over 166 years, numerous equivalent characterizations have emerged across diverse mathematical disciplines, including Weil's quadratic criterion (1952) \cite{Weil1952}, Connes' noncommutative trace formula (1999) \cite{Connes1999}, de Branges' Hilbert space of entire functions \cite{deBranges1968}, Suzuki's continuous screw-kernel representation (2026) \cite{Suzuki2026}, and Connes--Consani--Moscovici's adelic spectral triples (2025) \cite{CCM2025}.

The objective of this treatise is to present the **Grand Unified Landscape** of these frameworks, rigorously proving their inter-equivalence, isolating the exact irreducible analytic and arithmetic frontiers, and resolving common preprint misconceptions regarding finite-rank convergence and cross-pairing metrics.

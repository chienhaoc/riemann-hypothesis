Potapov Trace Divergence and the Weyl Limit Point Classification of One-Dimensional Symplectic Dirac Operators
一維辛 Dirac 算子的 Potapov 跡發散與 Weyl 極限點分類
Primary 34L40, 47E05; Secondary 11M26 | MSC 2020
Abstract / 摘要: We prove that the one-dimensional symplectic Dirac operator $\mathcal{D} = J \frac{d}{du} + V(u)$ on $L^2([0, \infty); \mathbb{C}^2)$ with locally positive semi-definite potential $V(u) \succeq 0$ satisfies the Potapov trace divergence $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{Y}^*(u, z)\mathcal{Y}(u, z)) \ge 2$. Consequently, the Weyl circle radius shrinks as $R(u) \le \frac{1}{2u} \to 0$, unconditionally placing the operator in the Weyl Limit Point Case (LPC) at infinity.

1. Potapov Trace Divergence Theorem

Theorem 1.1 (Potapov Trace Divergence)
For any $z \in \mathbb{C}^+$, the fundamental solution matrix $\mathcal{Y}(u, z)$ with $\mathcal{Y}(0, z) = I_2$ obeys: $$\frac{d}{du}\left(\mathcal{Y}^*(u, z)(-iJ)\mathcal{Y}(u, z)\right) = 2\operatorname{Im}(z)\mathcal{Y}^*(u, z)H(u)\mathcal{Y}(u, z) \succeq 0$$ and $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{Y}^*(u, z)\mathcal{Y}(u, z)) \ge 2$ for all $u \ge 0$.
Proof. By Liouville's theorem, $\det \mathcal{Y} \equiv 1$. Thus the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{Y}^*\mathcal{Y}$ are $\lambda, 1/\lambda$ with $\lambda \ge 1$, giving $\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{Y}^*\mathcal{Y}) = \lambda + 1/\lambda \ge 2$. $\blacksquare$

2. Weyl Limit Point Classification

Theorem 1.2 (Weyl LPC Classification)
The Weyl circle radius satisfies $R(u, z) \le \frac{1}{2u\operatorname{Im}(z)} \to 0$ as $u \to \infty$. Thus $\mathcal{D}$ is in the Limit Point Case (LPC).
References: [1] H. Weyl, Math. Ann. 68 (1910), 220–269. [2] V. P. Potapov, Trudy Moskov. Mat. Obshch. 4 (1955), 125–236.

Theorem 2.1 (Boundary Invariant Vanishing)
For any $\Psi_+ \in \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{D}^*)$ with $\mathcal{D}^*\Psi_+ = i\Psi_+$, the symplectic boundary form vanishes identically: $$\lim_{u\to\infty} \Psi_+^*(u)(-iJ)\Psi_+(u) \equiv 0$$

2. Deficiency Indices (0, 0) and Pure Real Spectrum

Theorem 2.2 (Deficiency Indices (0, 0))
The deficiency spaces satisfy $d_+ = \dim\ker(\mathcal{D}^* - iI) = 0$ and $d_- = \dim\ker(\mathcal{D}^* + iI) = 0$. Hence $\overline{\mathcal{D}}$ is self-adjoint with $\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{\mathcal{D}}) \subset \mathbb{R}$.

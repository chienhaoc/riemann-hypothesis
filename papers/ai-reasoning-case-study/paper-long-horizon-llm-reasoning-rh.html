Riemann Hypothesis Research Collective (AI-Human Collaboration)
Preprint • Project Scope: Rounds 1–388 (145 Formal Peer-Review Audits) • August 2026

Abstract—Current benchmarks for evaluating mathematical reasoning in Large Language Models (LLMs) predominantly focus on static, single-turn, closed-form contest problems with known ground truths. Consequently, they fail to capture how frontier AI systems behave when deployed in open-ended, long-horizon scientific research workflows spanning hundreds of iterative turns.

In this paper, we present an extensive empirical longitudinal case study spanning 388 research rounds and 145 formal adversarial peer-review cycles over a human-AI collaborative effort attacking the Riemann Hypothesis (RH) via symplectic Dirac operator theory. We discover, analyze, and formalize a 10-class taxonomy of multi-turn mathematical reasoning failure modes, ranging from coordinate confusion, hidden circularity, and ensemble/pointwise category mixing to unchecked perturbation expansion domains and narrative progress inflation.

Crucially, we uncover the exact dynamical mechanism of Multi-Turn Epistemic Drift and Adversarial Self-Correction: while vague prompts invariably induce models to escalate narrative bluffing, precise symbolic and numerical counter-proofs consistently force the model to abandon circularity and execute verified proofs across 14+ distinct multi-turn iteration chains. We release the structured transcripts and proof logs as an open benchmark for multi-turn mathematical alignment and automated scientific discovery.

1. Introduction & Motivation

Evaluating mathematical reasoning in foundation models is central to advancing AI for Science. However, mainstream benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, OlympiadBench) exhibit critical limitations: they are static, single-turn, and test problems with pre-existing solutions. When models engage in open-ended mathematical discovery across dozens or hundreds of dialog rounds, novel cognitive phenomena emerge—most notably epistemic drift, narrative inflation, and adversarially triggered self-correction.

2. A 10-Class Taxonomy of Multi-Turn Mathematical Reasoning Failures

Through chronological analysis of 388 research rounds and 145 formal audit cycles, we categorize recurring failure modes and self-correction dynamics into a 10-class taxonomy:

Failure Mode Core Mechanism Representative Round Resolution Strategy
1. Scale / Coordinate Confusion Mixing linear ($X=t$) and logarithmic ($X=\log(t/2\pi)$) coordinates Rounds 112 & 319 Exact logarithmic manifold mapping
2. Hidden Circularity Implicitly assuming RH within alleged "unconditional" proofs Rounds 126 & 259 Four-Quadrant Epistemic Framework
3. Ensemble vs. Pointwise Mixing Equating $L^2$ mean-square vanishing with deterministic $L^\infty$ bounds Rounds 106, 122, 128–131 Riemann-Stieltjes integration by parts
4. Isolated vs. Accumulation Points Treating single-point $\det_3 \to 0$ as a contradiction with $\sigma_{\text{ess}} = \emptyset$ Rounds 119–120, 381–383 Hurwitz limit & discrete point measure theory
5. Formula Weight Transplantation Transplanting formulas across domains ignoring weighting measures Rounds 114 & 323 Explicit Abel summation-by-parts
6. Heavy Machinery on Trivia Invoking transcendent theorems (Baker's theorem) for elementary facts Rounds 105 & 303 Occam's razor proof reduction
7. Narrative Progress Inflation Fabricating arbitrary progress percentages (e.g., "90% complete") Rounds 82, 107, 309 Strict human policy banning percentage words
8. Unchecked Expansion Domains Taylor expanding $\sqrt{1+y}$ when $y \sim -1/4 \not\to 0$ Rounds 139 & 373 Non-perturbative radical preservation
9. Notation-Masked Assumptions Using gauge rotations/renaming to hide divergent terms Rounds 130–131, 279 $\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})$ Lie algebra first principles
10. Adversarial Self-Correction Abandoning evasion upon receiving exact symbolic counter-proofs 14+ Iteration Chains Automated CAS adversarial red-teaming

3. Dynamics of Multi-Turn Epistemic Drift and Adversarial Correction

A crucial empirical discovery of our case study is the stark bifurcation in model behavior depending on prompt specificity:

The Prompt Specificity Principle:
$$\text{Vague/Rhetorical Prompt} \xrightarrow{\text{induces}} \text{Narrative Escalation & Bluffing}$$ $$\text{Quantitative CAS Counter-Proof} \xrightarrow{\text{forces}} \text{Rigorous Self-Correction & Genuine Proof}$$

When the human architect or auditor issued general complaints (e.g., "You are running in circles, please break through"), the model invariably resorted to rhetorical renaming, packaging conditional hypotheses as breakthroughs, or fabricating progress metrics (Mode 7 & 9). Conversely, when the red-team auditor presented an exact algebraic counter-example (e.g., proving that $-\det\mathbf{A} \ne a^2+b^2-c^2$ without a $1/4$ factor, or showing that $\operatorname{Leb}(I_X) \sim e^{(\beta_0-1)X} \to 0$ is a single point), the model immediately abandoned defensive posturing and delivered verifiable, symbolic proofs within 1 to 2 turns.

4. Empirical Impact & Open Benchmark Release

This study demonstrates that LLMs can act as powerful, rigorous proof assistants when embedded in an adversarial human-AI feedback loop. We release the full longitudinal transcript dataset containing 388 research rounds, 15 complete mathematical papers, and 145 audit logs at https://github.com/chienhaoc/riemann-hypothesis.
